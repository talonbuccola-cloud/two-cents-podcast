## You're Next (2011): The Family That Slays Together Should Probably Split the Inheritance

![You're Next theatrical poster](/assets/uploads/youre-next/poster.jpg){: style="float: right; max-width: 30%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 0 0.9em 1.1em;"}

Meeting your partner's family is stressful. Bringing a bottle of wine helps. Growing up on a survivalist compound helps considerably more.

*You're Next* is a 2011 home-invasion slasher about a wealthy family gathering for an anniversary dinner, three killers in animal masks, and one girlfriend who slowly realizes she is the only competent person in the house. It has crossbows, razor wire, sibling resentment, an industrial-strength blender, and a family so committed to arguing that not even an active massacre can keep them on topic.

In other words, it is exactly our kind of family movie.

### Looking for the Magic—and a Less Creepy Neighbor

The film opens next door, where a young woman named Talia has just finished having sex with Erik, a man old enough to make the exact nature of their arrangement feel like an unanswered question. He wanders around the house, drinks the beverage she thoughtfully left for him, and notices that the same song—Dwight Twilley Band's "Looking for the Magic"—is playing again. And again. Apparently she owns either a single-track CD or the most committed repeat button in Missouri.

Outside hangs a wind chime made from rusty silverware, the kind of decoration that looks less like folk art and more like somebody emptied a prisoner's collection of unfinished shivs onto a string.

Erik eventually finds "YOU'RE NEXT" written backward on the window in Talia's blood. A man in a lamb mask kills him, leaving the song to continue playing for anyone unfortunate enough to stop by and ask for milk.

That unfortunate person is Erin.

She has accompanied her boyfriend, Crispian Davison, to his parents' enormous country house for their wedding anniversary. His father Paul is a retired defense contractor. His mother Aubrey is already certain somebody is moving around upstairs. Crispian warns Erin that the family can be difficult, which is technically accurate in the same way that a crossbow bolt is technically pointy.

The rest of the Davisons arrive: older brother Drake and his wife Kelly; younger brother Felix and his aggressively unimpressed girlfriend Zee; and sister Aimee with her filmmaker boyfriend Tariq. Before dinner has properly begun, Drake is mocking Crispian's weight, interrogating Erin about having once been Crispian's student, and belittling Tariq's documentaries by explaining how much he personally enjoys advertisements.

The killers have not even attacked yet, and this dinner already needs an evacuation plan.

### The Dinner Conversation Gets Pointed

![The Davison family gathered around the dinner table](/assets/uploads/youre-next/dinner.jpg){: style="float: left; max-width: 48%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 1.1em 0.9em 0;"}

While the family screams over one another, Tariq notices movement outside. Nobody pays attention when he leaves the table and approaches the window. A crossbow bolt crashes through the glass and into his head.

That finally interrupts the argument. Briefly.

More bolts tear through the room, Drake is hit in the back, and everyone discovers that their cell signals are being jammed. Felix explains that illegal jammers can be bought online, so Drake—currently bleeding on the floor—uses what may be his final breaths to call his brother a lowlife for knowing that. It is one of the movie's funniest choices: these people cannot stop sniping at one another even while somebody outside is literally sniping at them.

Erin immediately begins doing everything the family should have done without being told. She gets everyone away from the windows, locks the doors, applies pressure around Drake's wound, looks for weapons, and tries sending a text to 911 in case it transmits when the signal returns.

The Davisons contribute by debating who can run the fastest.

Aimee volunteers. When nobody takes her seriously, she starts crying that no one in the family believes in her. Her father reassures her that he does, turning an escape plan during a mass shooting into the emotional resolution of a childhood grievance. The group decides to open the front door and let Aimee sprint for help.

She runs directly into a garrote wire stretched across the doorway.

For the record, she was fast. Speed just was not the problem.

### Please Listen to Erin

![Erin holding an axe inside the Davison house](/assets/uploads/youre-next/erin-axe.jpg){: style="float: right; max-width: 46%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 0 0.9em 1.1em;"}

After Aimee dies, the evening becomes a demonstration of what happens when nine people ignore the one woman with useful ideas.

Aubrey retreats to an upstairs bedroom by herself even though Erin specifically warns that isolating people makes them easier targets. A killer in a fox mask climbs out from under the bed and murders her. Kelly panics, bolts from the house, ducks under the wire, and races to the neighbors. She finds Erik's body and receives an axe to the head from Lamb Mask, who then sits on the couch with remarkably good posture.

Crispian announces that he is leaving to find help. He gives Erin a farewell speech, takes a weapon, and disappears into the darkness while his girlfriend stays behind to protect his family. We are aware that gender roles have evolved. We are also aware that abandoning your partner in a house full of murderers is a strange moment to demonstrate that progress.

The man in the tiger mask eventually gets inside and attacks Erin. This is where the film stops being a standard home-invasion story and becomes a very bad night for the home invaders. Erin stabs him, destroys his knee, and beats him to death with a meat tenderizer. She does not hesitate, deliver a speech, or leave him alive long enough to return in the third act. She checks the problem off the list and moves on.

Felix and Zee watch with the energy of two people waiting for somebody else to finish cleaning the kitchen.

Their behavior has been suspicious from the beginning. Whenever the family is in danger, they are conveniently elsewhere. Whenever somebody dies, they look less shocked than mildly inconvenienced. When Erin sarcastically thanks Felix for his help after killing Tiger Mask, he responds that she seemed to have it handled.

He is not wrong. He is, however, an asshole.

### An Inheritance With Administrative Problems

![Felix and Zee beneath the blood-written warning](/assets/uploads/youre-next/felix-and-zee.jpg){: style="float: left; max-width: 42%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 1.1em 0.9em 0;"}

Paul finds sleeping bags, food wrappers, and bottles of urine in an upstairs closet. The intruders have been hiding inside the house, which means this is not a random attack. He takes the discovery to Felix and Zee and is promptly killed by Fox Mask while the couple watches.

The plan finally comes into focus: Felix and Zee hired the masked men to kill the family so Felix could inherit his parents' fortune. They would rather organize a multi-person slaughter than wait for natural causes or develop employable skills.

Drake briefly becomes sympathetic just in time for Felix to tell him, with spectacularly bad bedside manner, that Kelly is dead. When Drake recoils, Felix stabs him with one tool after another and then complains that his brother is taking too long to die. Drake has spent most of the movie being unbearable, but discovering that your own brother arranged your murder is enough to earn at least a moment of pity.

Meanwhile, Zee asks Erin how she knows how to barricade rooms and build nail traps. Erin explains that she grew up on a survivalist compound in Australia. She has never told Crispian because, under normal circumstances, "my father trained me to defeat a coordinated home invasion" does not come up during casual dating conversation.

Tonight it becomes the most useful secret anyone in this family has ever kept.

Erin is thrown through a window and injures her leg, but she uses the woods, the darkness, a camera flash, nails, kitchen knives, and practically anything else within reach to turn the house against the attackers. One masked man steps onto her nail trap. Another follows a flashing camera into the basement and discovers that Erin can make more than one kind of mashed potato.

By the time the hired killers realize the random girlfriend is dismantling their entire operation, their biggest problem is no longer the police. It is payroll. One surviving attacker is furious that his brother has been killed; Felix tries to solve the dispute by promising him the dead man's share plus another hundred thousand dollars. Nothing says careful criminal planning like renegotiating contractor fees in a hallway full of corpses.

### Brain Smoothies and Other Relationship Enders

![Two of the animal-masked killers outside the house](/assets/uploads/youre-next/masked-killers.webp){: style="float: right; max-width: 42%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 0 0.9em 1.1em;"}

Once the masks are gone, Felix and Zee decide to kill Erin themselves. This goes about as well as everything else they have attempted.

Zee attacks. Felix stabs Erin in the back. Erin grabs a heavy glass blender, smashes it into Felix's head, drives the blades down, plugs it in, and makes what can only be described as a brain smoothie. She then pulls the knife from her own back and buries it in Zee's skull.

Again: no speeches. No mercy. No sequel bait. Just decisive household-appliance management.

Felix's phone rings. Erin answers without speaking, and Crispian's voice comes through asking whether everything is finished.

Of course Crispian is involved. His vanishing act was never heroism; he was preserving himself as the grieving survivor. Erin was supposed to be the innocent witness who could confirm that the entire Davison family had been attacked. When Crispian returns, he tries to sell her on the silver lining: his family is dead, but now he is rich, she is alive, and together they can pay off her alleged $500,000 in student loans.

Five hundred thousand dollars. Either Erin attended medical school on the moon or she took out a federal loan from every country on Earth.

Crispian insists he loves her. Erin responds by stabbing him in the neck and then the eye. It is the correct answer to both his proposal and the relationship in general.

Unfortunately, a police officer arrives at exactly the wrong second, sees Erin stabbing Crispian, and shoots her through the window. She survives and tries to warn him away from the front door, where she has rigged an axe to swing down when it opens.

He opens it.

The axe falls. The title appears. Family dinner is officially over.

### Why This One Works

*You're Next* understands that a room full of awful relatives can be funny without turning the threat into a joke. The violence is fast and vicious, but the family's inability to stop arguing gives the movie a streak of pitch-black comedy. Even after the crossbow attack begins, every old resentment remains available for immediate use.

The pacing helps. Aside from the early greetings and setup, the movie rarely slows down. Once dinner starts, one disaster folds into the next, and the reveals arrive before the premise can wear itself out. It has some of the same chaotic, wealthy-family energy as *Ready or Not*, with an equally capable woman discovering that the family she has entered is much worse than the people trying to kill her.

Most importantly, Erin is a fantastic final girl because the movie never requires her to become foolish to keep the plot moving. She makes sensible decisions, adapts when they fail, and treats every attacker as a problem that should remain solved. Her competence is so extreme that, for a moment, we wondered whether she might be the inside person. Then she tenderized a man's skull and cleared that up.

The score occasionally wanders into heavy synthesizer territory that feels imported from *A Nightmare on Elm Street* or *Stranger Things*. It does not ruin anything, but it sometimes sounds as if another decade has broken into the house.

The ending also split us slightly. Talon likes the shock of the officer shooting Erin and hopes the masks, bodies, camera evidence, and general state of the house will eventually support her story. Dan would have pushed the bleakness further: kill Erin with the first shot, let the officer trigger the axe, and leave absolutely no one standing. He cites *The Mist* as evidence, because apparently emotional devastation is funniest when it is efficiently timed.

### The Verdict

This is home-invasion horror with a sense of humor, a relentless pace, and one of the most capable final girls in the genre. The plot has a few moments that invite questions, but very little feels broken. More importantly, it is fun: a bloody, cynical family comedy in which the hired professionals discover they have invaded the wrong woman's weekend.

Talon gives *You're Next* eight cents. Dan gives it eight and a half.

Worth watching if you enjoy dysfunctional families, practical carnage, rich people making inheritance unnecessarily complicated, and heroines who understand that a blender can be both an appliance and a boundary.
