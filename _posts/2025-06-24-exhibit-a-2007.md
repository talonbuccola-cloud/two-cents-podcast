## *Exhibit A (2007)*: One Little Lie, One Terrible Pool, and a Family Completely Off the Rails

![Exhibit A 2007 movie poster featuring a camcorder sealed inside a police evidence bag](/assets/uploads/exhibit-a/poster.webp){: style="float: right; max-width: 30%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 0 0.9em 1.1em;"}

Some horror movies need a masked killer, a haunted house, or a demon with boundary issues.

*Exhibit A* needs one dad who lies about getting a promotion and then refuses, with almost heroic commitment, to simply admit he fucked up.

That is apparently enough.

### Welcome to the King Family Home Video
{: style="clear: none;"}

The first thing *Exhibit A* tells us is that what we are watching is evidence. The footage has been recovered by police, and teenage Judith King’s camcorder has become Exhibit A.

Then the movie rewinds to when the King family still looks normal.

Judith has just received the camera from her father, Andy, to replace an older one that broke. She immediately starts documenting the household: her mother Sheila, her older brother Joe, and Andy, an aggressively goofy dad who seems determined to turn ordinary family life into his own comedy special.

At first, they genuinely seem happy together. The Kings joke around, tease each other, and actually seem to enjoy being a family. Talon and Dan both thought that part worked especially well because the actors sell the affection before everything starts collapsing.

Judith is quieter than the rest of them and frequently aims the camera at Claire, the teenage girl next door. She has a crush on her but has not told her family, and the camera gives her a safe way to watch Claire from a distance without having to deal with the much more terrifying prospect of actually speaking to her.

At one point Claire comes into the yard and Judith hides under a table.

Not the camera.

Judith.

Even Talon initially thought she had only hidden the camera until Joe asked what she was doing under there.

Smooth.

![Andy and Sheila King embrace in their kitchen in Exhibit A 2007](/assets/uploads/exhibit-a/andy-sheila.webp){: style="float: left; max-width: 44%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 1.1em 0.9em 0;"}

The family also visits a much larger house Andy hopes they can buy if he gets an upcoming promotion. Judith is not thrilled about moving, partly because Claire has just entered her orbit. Andy brushes off her concern and promises she will love the new place.

Then comes the decision that destroys everything.

### Congratulations on the Promotion You Did Not Get
{: style="clear: none;"}

When Andy comes home from work, Sheila and the kids are waiting to surprise him.

Sheila asks if he got the promotion.

Andy initially says no.

And for one brief second, it looks like that is going to be the end of it.

Then he sees the disappointment on their faces and changes his answer.

Yes. Of course he got it.

Talon read this less as some carefully planned scam and more as a panicked lie born in the exact moment Andy realizes he has disappointed his family. He does not seem to walk through the door intending to deceive everybody. He says the truth, sees their reaction, and immediately reaches for a better version.

That better version becomes a nightmare because Andy never finds the reverse gear.

The family celebrates. The dream house is back on. Andy gets to remain successful provider and Man Who Has Everything Under Control.

Except he does not have the promotion.

How long did he think this could possibly work?

Eventually there will be a paycheck.

Numbers are famously difficult to gaslight.

Instead of admitting the lie, Andy starts trying to manufacture enough money and success to make it true after the fact. He becomes obsessed with improving their current home so they can sell it for more, including deciding, without consulting Sheila, to turn an unfinished pond area into a swimming pool.

They are trying to buy another house and need money for the deposit.

Andy responds by digging a swimming pool.

Financial planning has left the chat.

### The Pool of Increasingly Bad Decisions
{: style="clear: none;"}

Sheila is furious that Andy is spending money they need elsewhere.

Andy insists the pool will increase the property value.

Maybe.

What he eventually builds looks less like a luxury amenity and more like somebody buried a blue tarp and hoped Zillow would not zoom in.

The yard is torn apart. The paving is uneven. Andy spreads sand around it because it will be “like the beach,” which made Talon think that if he really wanted to recreate the rocky British beach from earlier, he should throw a few boulders around too.

Before the finished masterpiece, Andy’s unraveling starts showing up in smaller ways.

He tries to make a fake mishap video for a television clip show that offers prize money. Joe is supposed to back into the pool hole with a wheelbarrow and fall.

Joe does it.

Andy does not think it looks convincing enough.

So Joe does it again.

And again.

The joke turns uncomfortable as Andy becomes genuinely angry that his son is not falling into a hole correctly enough to make them money. Andy finally demonstrates it himself, gets hurt, and later pretends he did not.

Judith notices.

She notices a lot.

![Judith stands near the backyard shed while filming her family in Exhibit A 2007](/assets/uploads/exhibit-a/judith-backyard.webp){: style="float: right; max-width: 46%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 0 0.9em 1.1em;"}

At night she films Andy alone outside, smoking and acting increasingly agitated. She later discovers a trash bag stuffed with used scratch-off tickets.

That might be the most concise financial statement in the entire film.

As Dan pointed out, if you spend a hundred to win two, you did not win two. You lost ninety-eight.

Andy is now chasing money through a fake promotion, an unaffordable house, lottery tickets, and a home-video contest involving mild child endangerment.

Telling Sheila the truth remains completely free.

### Blood, Loans, and a Very Suspicious Coworker
{: style="clear: none;"}

The pressure around the dream house keeps getting worse.

Another offer comes in, and the amount needed to secure the property climbs. Eventually Sheila learns they need another £21,000 to make the deal work.

Andy agrees.

Of course he does.

Why stop lying when you can add debt?

Around the same period, Andy comes home covered in blood and tells Sheila that his friend and coworker Ray was brutally attacked with a crowbar while they were out together. Andy says he tried to help him and seems devastated.

Judith keeps watching.

Later she secretly records Andy trying to obtain loans over the phone, arguing because the lenders’ websites made borrowing money sound easier than reality is turning out to be.

When he realizes someone is outside the shed listening, he comes looking for the intruder. Judith hides, escapes, and runs back into the house.

The following day, Andy proudly unveils the finished swimming pool.

Sheila laughs.

Not happy laughter.

It is the kind of laughter that happens when screaming requires more energy.

Then the Kings host a pool party and open house. Everybody initially has fun, and somehow people use Andy’s questionable pool without immediately requiring a tetanus booster.

Andy, meanwhile, is trying far too hard to prove everything is wonderful.

Then Ray arrives.

Half his face is bandaged.

Party over.

### Ray Brings the Worst Possible Party Favor: The Truth
{: style="clear: none;"}

Ray publicly accuses Andy of attacking him.

Andy acts shocked and tries to get him away from the guests. Judith follows with the camera and records their confrontation.

That is where the lie finally breaks open.

Ray got the promotion.

Andy did not.

Ray believes Andy beat him with the crowbar because Andy wanted his job.

Suddenly the promotion, the house, the money problems, and Andy’s increasingly erratic behavior all click into place.

When Andy returns to the party, he desperately tries to recover the mood.

He dances.

A lot.

Talon loved how painfully awkward this became. Everyone else is standing around after watching a badly injured man accuse their host of a brutal crowbar attack, while Andy is practically crumping by himself and demanding that people have fun.

Nothing says “ignore the possible felony” like aggressive dancing.

Then Andy decides everyone should watch Judith’s camcorder footage.

Judith repeatedly begs him not to rewind it.

He does anyway.

Instead of showing everything the guests see, the movie cuts back to the King family’s happier day at the beach. Dan realized it was contrasting who they were with what they have become.

Afterward, Andy sits alone with the camera and blames it.

The camera ruined everything.

Not the fake promotion.

Not the crowbar.

The camera.

Excellent. We have identified the real criminal.

![Andy searches through his family’s private belongings in Exhibit A 2007](/assets/uploads/exhibit-a/andy-searching.webp){: style="float: left; max-width: 46%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 1.1em 0.9em 0;"}

### Everyone Has Secrets, So Apparently We Are Even
{: style="clear: none;"}

The dream-house plan has now collapsed, and the family remains in the home Andy was supposed to sell.

Andy decides that if the camera can expose him, it can expose everybody else too.

Before turning it on the rest of the family, however, Andy records the truth about Ray: he was the one who attacked him, hoping to take the promotion while Ray was incapacitated.

He starts searching the house for his family’s secrets.

His reasoning becomes spectacularly self-serving.

He finds a sex toy belonging to Sheila and treats it like evidence for the defense.

Talon could not get over the comparison. Andy has lied about a promotion, committed the family to a house they cannot afford, nearly destroyed their finances, and admitted attacking his coworker with a crowbar.

Sheila owns a dildo.

Case closed. Everybody is equally guilty.

He searches Joe’s room and finds drugs, resentment toward his father, and a private sexual recording involving Joe and a girl who appears to be Claire. Then he breaks open Judith’s locked belongings and discovers a collection of photographs and stolen items connected to Claire, finally realizing just how intense her crush has become.

When police come looking for him, Andy hides.

Then he creates his own family intervention.

He cuts the power, triggers the smoke alarm, locks the exits, and films everyone through night vision as they stumble around in the dark. He is so close to them at points that we could not understand how nobody hears him breathing.

His grand revelation is that everybody has secrets, so somehow his are less bad.

He forces the family to watch the footage he has assembled, outing Judith before she is ready and exposing Joe’s private life. When Sheila is horrified, Andy insists he is helping. He wants everybody to be honest so the family can heal.

His execution of good intentions continues to have the accuracy of a lawn sprinkler in a hurricane.

![Sheila is captured through night vision during the family confrontation in Exhibit A 2007](/assets/uploads/exhibit-a/night-vision.webp){: style="float: right; max-width: 46%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 0 0.9em 1.1em;"}

Then Sheila reveals the secret Andy did not find.

Years earlier, after having Joe, she became pregnant with Judith before she was sure she wanted another child. Andy immediately told everyone about the pregnancy, effectively taking the decision out of her hands.

Later, Sheila became pregnant a third time.

That time she did not tell Andy.

She had an abortion because she did not trust him to let the decision be hers.

The promotion did not create Andy’s controlling streak. It simply removed the structure that had been keeping it manageable.

The family tries to leave.

Andy refuses to let them.

During the chaos, Judith falls into the pool. She cannot swim, and Andy jumps in after her. She manages to reach the edge, where Sheila and Joe help her.

Andy beams.

“You swam!”

Of course he does.

His family is terrified of him, but look at the personal growth.

### The Camera Keeps Rolling
{: style="clear: none;"}

After the confrontation, Andy records himself alone.

He talks about loving his family, failing them, and wanting to set them free. His behavior strongly suggests he is preparing to kill himself.

Judith returns after talking with Claire and returning the things she took.

Andy tells her to leave him alone.

She does not.

She comes into the room, and for a moment the scene feels like it could become a reconciliation. Andy hugs her, tells her he loves her, tells her to go upstairs, then pulls her back for another embrace.

And begins suffocating her.

![Andy attacks Judith during the final confrontation in Exhibit A 2007](/assets/uploads/exhibit-a/andy-judith-final.webp){: style="float: left; max-width: 46%; height: auto; margin: 0.25em 1.1em 0.9em 0;"}

The scene goes on for an uncomfortably long time. The movie refuses the quick Hollywood version of suffocation. Judith fights. Andy cries. Her resistance weakens slowly until she stops moving.

Then Andy goes upstairs.

We hear blows.

He drags Joe’s body downstairs and lays him beside Judith.

Sheila appears, dazed and injured, sees her children, and begins screaming. Andy attacks her too, eventually dragging her body beside them while repeating loving words and insisting they are together now.

The man who began by trying to protect his family from disappointment has decided the solution is to destroy it.

Then he knocks the camera to the floor.

It lands near Judith.

The lens begins to fog.

She is breathing.

Andy hits the camera again, ending the recording before we learn whether Judith ultimately survives. His own fate is also left unresolved in the footage.

Then the film returns one final time to the beach.

The King family is together, laughing and ordinary.

Andy notices the camera is still recording.

And turns it off.

### The Verdict
{: style="clear: none;"}

This is where Talon and Dan finally split.

Talon appreciated the concept much more than the experience of watching it. She liked the idea that one incredibly simple lie could keep expanding until it consumed an entire family. The acting worked for her, especially the uncomfortable moments where Andy’s attempts at being funny or upbeat become painfully wrong.

But she found the movie slow.

Very slow.

There were several points where she checked how much time was left, and she felt the story could have been significantly shorter without losing its point. To her, *Exhibit A* plays more like a family drama built around horrific events than a horror movie designed to frighten or create sustained tension. She never found it scary and did not feel the anxiety she gets from movies such as *Eden Lake*.

Talking through it improved her opinion somewhat, but not enough to overcome how much it dragged while she was watching.

Talon gives *Exhibit A* **5 cents out of 10**.

Dan is on the other side.

He considers it one of his favorites and connected strongly with the psychological unraveling. He agreed that some of Judith’s repeated observations and a few minor scenes could have been trimmed, but he thought the gradual collapse was largely the point.

For Dan, the horror is how plausible the underlying mechanism is. People do make one stupid decision, lie to cover it, make another decision to protect the lie, and keep escalating until something that should have been manageable becomes catastrophic.

He also thought the performances convincingly sold both versions of the King family: the affectionate unit at the beginning and the frightened, fractured people at the end.

Dan gives *Exhibit A* **8 cents out of 10**.

So there it is.

A three-cent disagreement, one ruined family, one brutally assaulted coworker, one blue-tarp swimming pool, and thousands of pounds in problems that could have been avoided with six words:

“I didn’t get the promotion.”

Roll credits.
